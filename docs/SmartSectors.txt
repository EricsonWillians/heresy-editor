Smart Sector Designer
=====================

Smart Sector Designer is a persistent, review-first workspace for creating and
finishing sector geometry.  Enter 2D Sector mode and choose Edit -> Smart
Sector Designer, or open it from the Sector Operations menu.  The normal
sector panel is replaced until the designer is closed.

Modes
-----

Room creates rectangles with either press-drag-release or two clicks.  Hold
Shift for a square or Command/Ctrl to draw around the first point.

Polygon likewise accepts a center-to-radius drag or two clicks.  Its ordered
Profile selector starts with custom regular 3-through-64-sided geometry, then
triangle through dodecagon, 16/32/64/96-sided circles, 5/8/12/16-point stars,
Greek and Maltese crosses, 8/16/24-tooth gears, a 32-tooth sawblade,
trefoil through octofoil outlines, 8/16/24-lobe rosettes, and finally
192- and 288-vertex cathedral tracery.  Rotation applies relative to the
radius gesture.  The concave families expose a separate inner-radius
percentage.  Fixed profiles disable Sides because their topology is
intentional and deterministic.  The Review reports the final quantized
vertex count; integer formats can safely remove collinear detail.

Freeform accepts concave outlines and closes when its first anchor is clicked
or the plan is committed.

Extrude needs no prior selection.  Press a boundary directly, or press inside
a sector and drag through the boundary that should grow; an interior gesture
chooses the first wall crossed in the drag direction.  A deliberately grabbed
boundary can be extended into a selected nonbranching chain.  Drag sets depth
perpendicular to the chosen seam, so bent chains remain predictable.  Disable
it to use the exact signed Depth field; the sign selects the side and the
extrusion can be committed without placing a depth anchor.  Miter, bevel, and
segmented round joins are available.  Shift-click additional connected
boundaries before placing depth.

Inset / Ring offsets selected, nonadjacent sectors, including concave loops
and holes.  A sector can be targeted directly on the canvas; Shift/Ctrl-click
adds or removes more without changing the editor's real selection.  Its
conservative first-use offset avoids collapsing an ordinary 64-unit room.
While Inset owns the canvas, each mouse-wheel notch changes the absolute ring
thickness by one current grid step.  The wheel never silently crosses zero:
positive remains inward and a typed negative Offset remains outward.
Corridor accepts a start-to-end drag or clicked endpoints; further clicks add
manual waypoints.  Tab cycles the stable Straight, two L, and Z alternatives.
While Corridor owns the canvas, the mouse wheel changes Width by one current
grid step per notch instead of zooming the map; other modes retain normal
wheel zoom.  A routed corridor which returns to its starting sector keeps an
enclosed U-shaped middle as void (or as its pre-existing host space) instead
of painting that incidental loop as corridor floor.
Endpoint walls and doors stay associated with the endpoint where they were
picked, even when only one end touches existing geometry.
When a narrow corridor splits a longer existing wall, apply resolves the
actual post-split shared seam from its geometry and sector ownership.  Only
that portal receives Open or Wall flags and middle textures; the solid wall
segments on either side retain their original sidedef textures.

Stairs slice a dragged, automatically routed, or waypoint footprint into
steps, or finish a selected connected sector path.  Shift/Ctrl-click toggles
path sectors.  Plain clicks on an existing multi-sector path choose its start
and end, including the explicit endpoints required by a branch; numbered
preview labels show the resolved order.  Rise, count or tread sizing, a fitted
target floor, and preserved or fixed headroom are reviewed before applying.
Lift has an always-visible HOW LIFT WORKS guide and supports two deliberate
workflows.  For existing geometry, click the platform sector; Shift/Ctrl-click
adds disconnected or connected platform sectors.  For new geometry, drag
outward from a boundary to create a platform alcove.  Each connected component
receives one fresh tag.  The lowest adjacent floor is the inferred lower stop,
and the live Lift plan reports travel and trigger count before applying.
Trigger portals lists every usable platform boundary and permits exact
per-linedef inclusion.  Lift behavior is the semantic configuration preset
that supplies movement, activation and arguments.  Enter assigns the platform
tag and portal actions with the geometry in one Undo operation.

Architecture accepts a rectangular footprint either initiated inside one
ordinary host sector or wholly in clear void.  Inside a room it retains the
host and inserts only supports that stay within that host; the footprint may
meet the room edge because Margin already controls the actual sections.  In
particular, the first press locks the host before grid snapping, so a layout
can surround holes or follow a strongly concave room even when the footprint
center is not part of that sector.  In void it first creates a walkable host
hall, then inserts every support into that plan-local host in the same Undo
operation.  A void hall may not touch or consume existing geometry.

Element offers 16 purpose-specific layouts: a single pillar, paired pillars,
triumphal-arch piers, corner piers, colonnade, double-row arcade, perimeter
cloister, hypostyle hall, buttressed bay, flying-buttress array, four-row nave,
cruciform transept, columned apse, radial rotunda, multi-ring sanctuary, and
fortified keep.  Bays controls repeated stations where the chosen layout uses
them; Structure size controls the support section and Margin protects
circulation at the footprint edge.  Functional square, Classical round,
Romanesque octagonal, Gothic clustered, Industrial geared, Art Deco sunburst,
and Infernal star sections are portable straight-lined constructions in Doom,
Hexen, and UDMF.

While Architecture owns the canvas, each wheel notch changes Structure size
by one quarter of the current grid step.  The control respects a
style-specific, format-safe minimum, so intricate clustered or pointed
sections cannot silently collapse into multiple cells; choosing a more
detailed style raises a smaller current size to that minimum automatically.
Every support is a closed sector inheriting its existing or newly generated
host context.
Overlapping supports, host-boundary crossings, protected functional hosts,
existing geometry contact, and ambiguous topology block apply.
The white footprint envelope and cyan structural sections remain visible
throughout review.  A blocked layout does not disappear: its intended
footprint and affected supports turn red, and exact invalid points or
conflicting existing lines receive red markers.  Selecting the issue centers
the canvas on that exact preview location.

Connections
-----------

Extrusion bases, corridor endpoints, and generated stair endpoints can be
Open, Wall, or Door.  Open creates an ordinary traversable portal.  Wall
retains a blocking, textured seam.  Door inserts a minimum-depth sector and
invokes the same format-aware Smart Door planner, preset, arguments,
activation, orientation, and Face / Track inference as the standalone tool.
A generated door and its surrounding geometry are one Undo operation.

For extrusion, Auto longest places one door on the longest source segment,
using the lowest linedef number as a stable tie-breaker.  Selected segments
can instead place independent doors along one or more chain edges.  Door width
zero chooses the widest safe opening and automatically reserves eight-unit
track walls at chain bends; an explicit width is centered in that safe span
and Door offset moves it along the segment.  Door depth controls the track
length.  The review blocks widths below eight units, openings that consume a
bend's track wall, and depths that leave no usable sector behind.  Non-door
portions of a Door extrusion base remain blocking walls.

If the active game and format has no compatible door preset, Door is disabled
while Open and Wall remain available.  Lift mode behaves similarly when no
verified lift preset can be represented.

Review and safety
-----------------

Every gesture is quantized before validation: integer map units in Doom and
Hexen maps, and 16.16 precision in UDMF.  Preview and apply use those same
coordinates.  The overlay distinguishes proposed and retained geometry,
openings, doors, tracks, stairs, lifts, architecture, cuts, anchors, warnings,
and conflicts.
Hatched fills remain readable in both OpenGL and software rendering.

The Review section reports sectors, planned splits, doors, steps, lifts, and
architectural structures.
Selecting an issue centers the map on its affected sector, line, or preview
position.  Copy Review copies the complete summary and every untruncated
diagnostic.  Expand Review opens the same text in a resizable, wrapped view
where any portion can be selected and copied; an attempted Enter/Make Sectors
commit opens this view automatically when blocked.  Blocking errors disable
Make Sectors.  Apply always plans again, then commits through one EditOperation;
a stale or invalid plan produces no partial edit and no Undo record.

Existing vertices and exact segments are reused.  Proper crossings are split,
while partial collinear overlap, nonmanifold or self-intersecting outlines,
zero-area and snapped-away geometry, slivers, ambiguous generated overlaps,
and unsafe coordinates are blocked.  Existing action lines and recognized
door or lift sectors are protected.  A local door or lift trigger shared by an
ordinary neighboring room protects the functional sector, not that unrelated
neighbor.  Replace affected sectors is an explicit opt-in for operations which
intentionally take ownership of protected space.

Properties
----------

Floor, ceiling, and light can inherit automatically or use absolute or
relative values.  Empty floor, ceiling, wall, Face, and Track texture fields
mean Auto.  Unknown resource names warn without blocking, which allows custom
resources that have not yet been loaded.

Floor flat, Ceiling flat, and Wall texture each have a searchable Choose
button, a clickable live preview tile, and a green Auto control.  Flat pickers
list loaded flats and Wall lists loaded wall textures.  Auto previews the
current contextual inheritance before applying.  In Inset mode these choices
belong to the selected Ring or Inner result model independently.

For a Door connection, Door face is the moving slab surface and Track wall is
the narrow side wall inside the doorway.  Choose Face / Choose Track, or click
their preview tiles, to search the currently loaded wall textures.  The same
picker and Auto semantics are used by the standalone Smart Door review.  Auto
first infers from the source portal or wall, falls back deterministically, and
shows the resolved texture names and tiles before applying.

Special and Tag are optional exact sector values.  Special remains a manual
numeric field for custom port effects, and Choose Special opens the searchable
list declared by the active game configuration.  A read-only Meaning row shows
the selected description, Auto inheritance, or that a manual value is
undeclared.  Empty fields inherit the model sector for new geometry and
preserve existing sectors while finishing.  Malformed or out-of-format values
block applying; undeclared nonzero specials produce a warning so port-specific
custom effects remain possible.

Inset mode's Edit selector keeps independent property models for the ring and
the inner result, so either side can inherit context or receive its own
heights, lighting, and flats.

Extruded Smart Doors
--------------------

In Extrude mode, set Base seam to Smart Door.  The designer inserts the
door-depth sector at the chosen source segment, finishes it with the selected
Smart Door behavior, and creates the remaining extrusion behind it.  Door
geometry and the room extrusion share one atomic Undo step.

An existing action on the chosen extrusion seam is connection-owned rather
than treated as geometry hidden inside the new sector.  Open and Wall preserve
that action and its arguments; Smart Door warns and deliberately replaces it
with the selected door behavior.

Door face and Track wall each have a persistent green Auto toggle.  Auto clears
the typed override and re-infers that texture for the current doorway.  The
review immediately replaces the override with the actual inferred name and
preview tile, and the atomic apply consumes that same resolved choice.  Choose
opens the loaded-texture browser.  Door width zero chooses the widest safe
opening, while Door lines can target the longest segment automatically or
explicit chain segments.

Extrusion gesture and direction
-------------------------------

Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+S opens Smart Sector Designer from Sector mode.  X opens it
directly in Extrude mode; X is also the Smart extrude entry in Sector
Operations.

Press on a boundary or anywhere inside its sector and drag toward the space
that should contain the new sector.  Starting inside chooses the crossed wall,
even when another nearby wall lies inside the normal hover radius.  Releasing
the primary mouse button locks the preview depth; Enter, Space, or Make Sectors
applies it.  A click without movement keeps the click-to-place workflow
available.  Shift-click adds connected boundary lines, and pressing any
selected member begins a new drag measured from that seam.

The initial press locks direction measurement to its chosen seam.  This
prevents bent chains from switching sides as the pointer moves, and linedef
winding never reverses pointer intent.
A white normal ruler and numeric depth sit over the filled result.  Opposite
side (F) is an explicit panel control: unchecked always follows the pointer;
checked deliberately uses the other side.  It resets for each new gesture.

Controls and repeat workflow
----------------------------

* Left-click places an anchor; Room, Polygon, Architecture, Corridor, and
  generated Stairs also accept press-drag-release.
* In Extrude and wall-alcove Lift, press-drag-release locks depth.
* Inset and Lift accept canvas sector targets.  Shift/Ctrl-click toggles a
  batch; selected Stairs use plain clicks to set path direction.
* Right-click removes the last anchor.
* Enter or Space applies a valid plan.
* Escape clears the active gesture; Escape again exits while idle.
* Tab cycles automatic corridor routes.
* In Corridor mode, the mouse wheel changes Width by the current grid step.
  In Inset / Ring, it changes thickness while preserving inward/outward intent.
  In Architecture, it changes Structure size by one quarter grid step and
  clamps at the current style's safe minimum.
* f or F toggles the visible Opposite side state or reverses the corridor.
* Shift+G toggles Snap to Grid; lowercase g only shows or hides the grid.
* Drag the grip on the designer's left edge to widen or narrow the side area.
  The map keeps a protected minimum width; double-click the grip to reset it.

View -> Snap to Grid is a shared, checked setting for ordinary sector drawing
and Smart Sector Designer.  When enabled, anchors and live drag points snap to
the current grid after Shift's angle constraint is applied.  Turning it on or
off refreshes the active preview immediately and the explicit choice is
restored on the next application launch.  Format quantization remains a final
safety step even when grid snapping is disabled.

Keyboard gestures are consumed while the designer owns the 2D canvas.  The
designer stays active after apply, selects the resulting sectors, and starts a
fresh gesture even when nothing was selected beforehand.  Each apply has its
own Undo record.  A blocked preview shows its error count and Make Sectors
identifies and navigates to the first exact reason without mutating the map.
The expanded review remains nonmodal, so it can stay open while the gesture is
adjusted and updates live with the new plan.  After a successful apply it
closes, and repeat mode reports WAITING FOR GESTURE with zero errors until the
next gesture begins.  Enter while waiting only repeats the placement guidance;
it never reports a failed operation or creates an edit.
Selection-based Inset, Stairs, and Lift commits deliberately wait for a new
canvas target afterward, so an accidental second Enter cannot apply the
operation twice.  Mode changes are atomic: the visible mode, its fields,
gesture ownership, target sectors, route, door/lift choices, preview, and
status guidance change together.  Map changes discard stale object references.
Mode changes, map changes, closing the panel, cancellation, and exceptions
clear the transient preview.

Configuration
-------------

Door behavior comes from `door_preset`.  Lift behavior comes from:

    sector_action_preset lift <id> "<label-or-@special>" <special> \
        <encoded|use_once|use_repeat> <arg1> <arg2> <arg3> <arg4> <arg5>

Lift arguments are integers or `@tag`.  Doom encoded presets assign their
fresh tag to the platform sector and cannot use `@tag`.  Hexen and UDMF use
presets require exactly one `@tag`.  Repeating a kind/ID replaces it without
changing order, and `clear lines` clears both line definitions and semantic
sector-action presets.

Boundaries
----------

Curves are portable straight-line approximations.  Remote switches, scripted
sequences, slopes, 3D floors, polyobjects, prefab libraries, terrain
generation, and destructive automatic merging remain outside this tool.
